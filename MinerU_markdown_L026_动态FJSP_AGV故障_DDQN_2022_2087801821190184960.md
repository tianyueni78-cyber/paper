In this study, the dynamic joint scheduling problem for processing machines and transportation robots in a flexible job shop is investigated. The study aims to minimize the order completion time (makespan) of a job shop manufacturing system. Considering breakdowns, order insertion and battery charging maintenance of robots, an event-driven global rescheduling strategy is adopted. A novel memetic algorithm combining genetic algorithm and variable neighborhood search is designed to handle dynamic events and obtain a new scheduling plan. Finally, numerical experiments are conducted to test the effect of the improved operators. For successive multiple rescheduling, the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm is verified by comparing it with three other algorithms under dynamic events, and through statistical analysis, the results verify the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm. 

Keywords: flexible job shop, dynamic joint scheduling, battery charging maintenance, multi-agent 

## 1. Introduction

Owing to the higher flexibility and robustness of the production process, the flexible job shop scheduling problem (FJSP) has attracted increasing attention. In a flexible job shop, for each job operation, more than one machine is available to process the operation (eligible machines), which increases the scheduling difficulty. Because each job must be processed on multiple machines, which means that transportingjobs between stations is unavoidable, using robots, mostly automatic guided vehicles (AGVs), to transport jobs is an ideal strategy. Moreover, unexpected events, such as machine breakdowns and urgent order insertion may occur at any time, and one-time static scheduling cannot satisfy the production needs of factories. Considering a variety of dynamic event handling strategies and rescheduling methods, this study examines a dynamic joint flexible job shop scheduling problem (DJFJSP) to realize real-time scheduling which has become a focus of job shop intelligence. 

Regarding scheduling problems, many scholars regard machining and transportation as two independent processes for two-stage scheduling. Zhao et al. [1] proposed a two-stage cooperative evolutionary algo rithm with problem-specific knowledge to address the no-wait flow-shop problem. Zhao et al. [2] proposed a self-learning discrete Jaya algorithm to address the energy-efficient distributed no-idle flow-shop scheduling problem. Zhou et al. [3] developed a self-adaptive differential evolution algorithm to solve the single-batch-processing machines scheduling problem. 

However, because a transfer process always exists between the two operations, the processing process is closely coupled with the transportation process. To reduce the production cost and shorten the production cycle, realizing the joint scheduling of processing machines and transportation tools is necessary. Ham et al. [4] proposed an application of constraint programming and a medium-scale benchmark instance for joint job shop scheduling, and they studied the joint flexible job shop scheduling problem (JFJSP) [5] based on this. Homayouni et al. [6] proposed a mixed integer linear programming model and a local search algorithm based heuristic to address the JFJSP. Xu et al. [7] proposed a dynamic algorithm based on simulation approach and multi-objective optimization. Yan et al. [8] designed a three-layer encoding with redundancy and decoding with correction to improve the genetic algorithm (GA) and solve the JFJSP. Li et al. [9] considered an energy-efficient FJSP with transportation and sequence-dependent setup times, and developed an imperialist competitive algorithm with feedback to minimize the makespan. 

The current research on job shop rescheduling focuses on two main strategies: right-shift rescheduling and complete rescheduling. Right-shift rescheduling does not change the original scheduling scheme, but only ad justs according to dynamic events, enabling a fast reaction speed but a poorer effect. Complete rescheduling is the rescheduling of production tasks after dy namic events, which is slower but yields satisfactory re sults. In terms of dynamic problems, Ren et al. [10] proposed a proactive-reactive methodology and improved particle swarm optimization for new arrival jobs. Fang et al. [11] proposed scheduling resource parameter updating methods and dynamic interactive scheduling strategies to achieve real-time precise scheduling. Baykasoglu et al. [12] presented a constructive algorithm that can solve the dynamic flexible job shop scheduling problem (DFJSP) with machine capacity constraints and sequence-dependent setup times, and employed a greedy randomized adaptive search procedure. Cao et al. [13] designed an adaptive scheduling algorithm for new jobs continuously arriving to address makespan minimization. Zhang et al. [14] proposed a two-stage genetic programming hyperheuristics framework with feature selection to automatically evolve scheduling heuristics using only the selected features for the DFJSP. Yonemoto et al. [15] proposed an “adaptive manipulation” procedure for establishing a reactive revision policy to establish an automated scheduling technique that responds flexibly to unforeseen events. Tian et al. [16] proposed a dynamic job-shop scheduling model based on deep learning for the DFJSP. Chen et al. [17] constructed a mathematical model of fuzzy FJSP, proposed a mixed quantum algorithm based on a local optimization strategy and improved the optimization rotation angle. Zhang et al. [18] proposed a novel multifidelity-based surrogate-assisted genetic programming. He et al. [19] designed an improved memetic algorithm (MA) to solve the DFJSP by considering the integrated scheduling of machines and AGVs. 

Hence, the DJFJSP is investigated in this study for the following reasons. First, the processing and transportation processes are closely coupled and jointly affect the total production time (makespan); therefore, the joint scheduling is significant. Second, in an actual job shop, the occurrence of dynamic events, such as machine breakdowns, AGV breakdowns, and emergency order insertions, hinder the production process, which highlights the importance of real-time scheduling. Moreover, battery power constraints and charging maintenance of AGVs have often been ignored by researchers [20]. Because the classic job shop scheduling problem (JSP) has been proven to be an NP-hard problem [21], and the DJFJSP is an extension of the JSP, the DJFJSP is also an NP-hard problem. 

Compared with traditional JSPs, in the DJFJSP, machine breakdowns and other events require timely responses to ensure the safety and smooth production of the job shop. Therefore, the dynamic problem is stricter on time. A rule-based constructive heuristic method can quickly obtain a scheduling scheme. However, the DJFJSP contains three sub-problems, including the selection of processing machines and AGVs, the processing sequence of machines and the transport sequence of AGVs, and adds constraints such as AGV charging and maintenance, making it difficult to establish accurate and effective rules. The local search algorithm is faster, but finding an optimal solution is difficult. The global search algorithm is inferior to the local search algorithm in terms of convergence time. Considering the search time and optimization of results, this study adopts an MA combining global and local search to solve the DJFJSP. 

The DJFJSP requires the simultaneous scheduling of processing machines and AGVs, including the above three sub-problems; therefore, the tri-string coding method is used. Because of the characteristics of this problem, the crossover and mutation operators of a GA can easily operate with corresponding constraint rules for tri-string coding; therefore, the GA is used as the global search algorithm. In local search algorithms, the variable neighborhood search (VNS) algorithm can design a variety of neighborhood operators according to the characteristics of machine breakdowns and other problems, and it can change the search range through neighborhood structures for deep exploitation, which has good flexibility and can jump out of the local optimal solution. 

Based on this analysis, this study improves the MA to solve the DJFJSP. The main contributions of the study are as follows: 

(1) Under a variety of dynamic events, machines and AGVs are jointly scheduled to realize flexible job shop scheduling based on the event-driven global rescheduling strategy. 

(2) A flexible job shop mathematical model for joint scheduling is proposed by considering the battery charging maintenance problem of AGVs. 

(3) For the MA based on a GA and VNS, several improvement strategies have been proposed, including improving the crossover and mutation operators to prevent the generation of infeasible solutions, designing some specific neighborhood operators according to the characteristics of the problem, and integrating the coupling relationship between some local search operators to maintain the superiority of the solutions. 

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the description and formulation of the DJFJSP. Section 3 introduces the proposed MA and comparison algorithms. Section 4 describes the experiments conducted and analyzes the results. Finally, Section 5 presents conclusions and directions for future work.

## 2.1. Problem Description

The DJFJSP can be described as follows. As shown in Fig. 1, there exist m machines $M = \{ M _ { 1 } , M _ { 2 } , . . . ,$ $M _ { m } \}$ , x AGVs $V = \{ V _ { 1 } , V _ { 2 } , \ldots , V _ { x } \}$ , and n jobs J = $\{ J _ { 1 } , J _ { 2 } , \ldots , J _ { n } \}$ . The initial positions of the jobs are located in the raw material warehouse. After completing one job operation, the job must be transported to the machine of the next operation by an AGV. A job is transported to the product storeroom when all job operations are processed. The AGVs move along the path in the map, and the Dijkstra algorithm is used to plan the shortest path between sites. Before the AGV performs a task, it must ensure that the current power is sufficient to complete the task. If the power is insufficient, the AGV goes to the nearest charging point before performing the transportation task. Random events occur during job shop production, including machine breakdowns, AGV breakdowns and order insertions. The entire task completes once all jobs are transported to the product storeroom.

To simplify the DJFJSP, the following assumptions are considered in this problem: 

(1) The sequence of operations for a job cannot be changed, and there is no sequence constraint between different jobs. 

(2) Each machine can process only one job at a given time, and each AGV can transport one job at a given time. 

(3) The process cannot be interrupted after starting, except in special circumstances, such as machine breakdowns. 

(4) The AGVs maintain a constant speed during operation and charge at the nearest charging point when their battery power is insufficient to perform the current task. 

(5) The routing conflict of AGVs is not considered.

## 3. Memetic Algorithm for the DJFJSP

In this section, an MA combining GA and VNS is designed for the DJFJSP. 

## 3.1. Encoding and Decoding

In the proposed DJFJSP, the three resources to be scheduled are represented as operation, machine and AGV strings. Fig. 2 illustrates tri-string coding. The machine string is divided into sections according to the number ofjobs and operations. Each number in the string represents the machine serial number of the corresponding operation. The operation string uses a coding method based on the operation. The numbers in the string represent the job number. The same location of the operation and AGV strings represents a corresponding relationship in which the AGV is responsible for transporting the operation. For example, the first gene in the operation string represents the first operation of Job 3, which is transported by AGV 1 and processed by Machine 1. 

## 3.2. Dynamic Scheduling Flowchart

The dynamic scheduling of a flexible job shop requires that when dynamic events occur in the job shop production process and disturb the current production scheduling plan, a timely reschedule is required according to the current job shop situation, and production continues according to the new scheduling plan.

This study considers the rescheduling of three dy namic events: machine breakdowns, AGV breakdowns, and emergency order insertions. This study uses an event-driven global rescheduling strategy. Rescheduling aims to obtain the job shop resource states at the time of events, including the processing states of the machines, operation states of AGVs, and completion degree of jobs, and modify these according to the relevant constraints of the actual job shop to obtain the initial state for rescheduling. A key constraint is that the fault-free robot executing a task cannot be interrupted, that is, the fault-free machine in the process can only be rescheduled after completing the current operation, and the fault-free AGV running on the road can only be rescheduled after reaching is destination. ## 3.3. Memetic Algorithm

In metaheuristic algorithms, the crossover and mutation operators of the GA can easily operate with the corresponding constraint rules for tri-string coding, including the operation, machine, and AGV strings. The VNS can change the search range through neighborhood structures for deep exploitation, has good flexibility, and can jump out of the local optimal solution. Therefore, this study uses the MA combining the GA and VNS to solve the proposed problem. 

## 3.3.1. Initialization

A random initialization method with certain constraints is used to ensure that each chromosome individual of the population represents a feasible solution. The three coding strings of an individual are generated according to different rules. For the operation string, gene hashes are generated according to the number of jobs and oper ations, which are randomly arranged to obtain the operation string. The AGV string is obtained by evenly al locating transportation tasks to all AGVs. The machine string represents the processing machine determined for each operation. For each operation, the machine can only be selected randomly from the corresponding set of eligible machines.

## 3.3.2. Selection Operation

The purpose of the selection operation is survival of the fittest, and individuals that have the likeliest opportunity to reproduce have higher fitness values. The binary tournament method is used to select the parent individuals to produce the next generation. The parent and offspring populations generated according to the crossover and mutation operations are combined into a temporary population. Iteratively, two individuals are randomly selected from the temporary population for comparison. The individual with the higher fitness value survives and enters the population of the next generation. 

## 3.3.3. Crossover Operation

The crossover operation enhances the exploration ability of the algorithm. A two-stage crossover mechanism is developed for tri-string coding. The precedence preserving order-based crossover (POX) [22] is used for the operation string. According to the characteristics of the 

DJFJSP, both machines and AGVs may be unavailable for a long time; therefore, the number of tasks they need to undertake should be variable. Therefore, in the first stage, both the machine and AGV strings use a uniform crossover operator [23]. In the second stage, the machine string randomly selects a segment to cross, and the AGV string uses a POX operator. 

## 3.3.4. Mutation Operation

Mutation operations enhance the random search ability of the algorithm and maintain the diversity of the population genes. Similar to the crossover operation, the three coding strings use different mutation operators according to their characteristics. For the operation and AGV strings, two genes in the string are randomly selected and their positions are exchanged. For the machine string, a gene in the string is randomly selected and stochastically replaced in the eligible machine set of this operation to ensure the generation of a feasible solution. 

## 3.3.5. Operators of Variable Neighborhood Search

The VNS [24] is an individual-based local search algorithm. When the VNS searches based on a better initial solution obtained by the global search algorithm, it is more conducive to find the optimal solution via a deep search near a better solution. 

For the machine string, to ensure that the generated individuals are feasible solutions, a gene is stochastically selected, and the machine of the corresponding operation is randomly replaced in its eligible machine set. 

For the operation and AGV strings, three local search operators are used in this study: relocation, exchange, and 2-opt. 

• Relocation randomly selects a gene and inserts it into another position in the string. 

• Exchange stochastically selects two genes and exchanges their positions. 

• 2-opt randomly selects a segment of the string and reverses it in place. 

In addition, the operator can be improved according to the characteristics of the problem. First, if a job does not appear in the first 3/2n of the process segment (n is the number of jobs), a gene representing the job is randomly removed and inserted into the above segment. Second, the AGV string acts with the operation string to protect the association relationship between the genes at the same position of the operation and AGV strings after each population iteration.

## 4.1. Experimental Setup

Because this paper proposes the DJFJSP based on dual resource scheduling, MK01-15 proposed by Brandimarte [25] are extended as test instances (EMK01-15) in the paper. The number of AGVs is determined according to the total number of operations, and the ratio between them is (0.2, 0.25). The number of charging piles relates to the scale of the map, and the ratio of the number of charging piles to the number of map nodes is (0.06, 0.08). The job shop layout is described in Section 2.1, and the driving routes of the AGVs between two sites are determined by the Dijkstra algorithm. The crossover probability, mutation probability, and population size are 0.95, 0.05, and 60, respectively. The battery capacity and unit charging rate of the AGVs are 120 and 8, respectively. The unit loaded and no-load power consumption rates are set to 3 and 1. In the experiments, Eclipse is used as the development tool and the computer is equipped with an Intel<sup>®</sup> core<sup>™</sup> i5-7200U CPU @ 2.50 GHz, 2.71 GHz. 

## 4.2. Algorithm Analysis Experiments

Orthogonal experiments were conducted to verify the effectiveness of the improved operators proposed in Section 3. On the one hand, for the improved crossover mechanism in Section 3.3.3, only the operators of the first stage used (Cross FS), only the operators of the second stage used (Cross SS) and the improved two-stage crossover mechanism (Cross TS) in the iterative process are for the comparative experiments. On the other hand, the original and improved neighborhood operators (ND ori and ND pro) in Section 3.3.5 were compared. Fig. 4 shows the results based on the makespan. 

The results verify the effectiveness of the improved operators. For the crossover operators, owing to the existence of processing machine breakdowns, AGV breakdowns and AGV charging maintenance, these events have significant requirements on charging the task volume of the processing machines and AGVs. Therefore, the first-stage operators are used to adapt to the large changes in the number of tasks caused by dynamic events. The second-stage operator is convenient for adjusting the task execution order. For neighborhood operators, because the previous operations of the jobs directly affect the processing of subsequent operations, ensuring that the previous operations are processed earlier can effectively reduce the makespan in some cases.

## 5. Conclusion

This study addresses the DJFJSP by considering dynamic events including machine breakdowns, AGV breakdowns and order insertions. First, to minimize completion time, a mathematical model for DJFJSP is proposed, which considers the constraints of machine scheduling, AGV scheduling and AGV battery charging maintenance. Second, adopting the tri-string coding method, an improved MA based on a GA and VNS is pro posed, which makes improvements according to the characteristics of the problem. A two-stage crossover mechanism is proposed and a coupling relationship is added between neighborhood structures to maintain the superiority of the solutions. Third, the validity of the operators is tested. Successive rescheduling experiments are used to test the performance of the GA, VNS, BRKGA, and MA to find optimal solutions, which conclude that the MA can effectively solve the DJFJSP and is superior to the other algorithms. 

Future works should incorporate more important factors in the flexible job shop, such as job shop energy consumption and the AGV conflict-free routing problem.
